H7/g6 fit:

01 / THE NOTATION

What does H7/g6 mean?

The two classes describe separate parts of the same fit. Uppercase H7 is the hole; lowercase g6 is the shaft. The letters place their tolerance zones relative to the basic size, and the numbers specify the IT grades that determine the widths of those zones.

HOLEH7

H: lower deviation is zero.
7: tolerance width is IT7.

SHAFTg6

g: upper deviation is negative.
6: tolerance width is IT6.

This is a hole-basis clearance fit. Even the largest permitted shaft is smaller than the smallest permitted hole. The codes do not specify one fixed clearance: you also need the basic size to look up the deviations.

For example, at 20 mm, H7/g6 gives 7–41 µm of diametral clearance. At 50 mm, it gives 9–50 µm. The difference comes from the size intervals used for IT widths and fundamental deviations.

02 / WORK IT THROUGH

50 mm H7/g6: from deviations to limits

Exactly 50 mm belongs to the interval over 30 mm, up to and including 50 mm. The H7 hole has deviations of 0 to +25 µm. The g6 shaft has deviations of −25 to −9 µm. Convert micrometres to millimetres by dividing by 1,000.

H7 HOLE LIMITS50.000–50.025 mm

50 + 0.000 to 50 + 0.025

g6 SHAFT LIMITS49.975–49.991 mm

50 − 0.025 to 50 − 0.009

Calculate the smallest and largest possible gaps

Minimum clearancesmallest hole − largest shaft50.000 − 49.991 = 0.009 mm

Maximum clearancelargest hole − smallest shaft50.025 − 49.975 = 0.050 mm

The result is 0.009–0.050 mm, or 9–50 µm. These are diameter differences. For ideal concentric cylinders, the radial gap on each side is half that amount: 4.5–25 µm. A shaft resting against one side does not have equal gaps around its circumference.

The hole tolerance width is 25 µm and the shaft width is 16 µm. Their sum, 41 µm, is the spread between minimum and maximum clearance: 50 − 9 = 41 µm.

Watch the interval boundary.

50.000 mm uses the over-30-to-50 interval. At 50.001 mm, the next interval applies: the H7/g6 deviations become 0/+30 µm for the hole and −29/−10 µm for the shaft, giving 10–59 µm clearance. Do not round a basic size across a boundary before calculating.

04 / A NEARBY FIT

H7/g6 vs H7/h6

The hole stays H7 in both fits. Changing the shaft from g6 to h6 moves its upper limit to the basic size. At 50 mm, both shaft zones are 16 µm wide, but they occupy different positions.

Comparison at a basic size of 50 mm

H7/g6 ↗49.975–49.9919–50

H7/g6 preserves a positive minimum dimensional clearance. H7/h6 allows zero minimum clearance at the limiting sizes. H7/h6 is still classified as a clearance fit; it does not guarantee a freely sliding assembly under every physical condition.

05 / FROM NUMBERS TO PARTS

What should you check before choosing H7/g6?

H7/g6 is useful to consider when mating parts need a small positive dimensional clearance, for example in a removable locating or sliding arrangement. Whether that clearance works depends on the actual assembly.

  • Motion and load. Assess the needed guidance, play, speed, load and lubrication. A size fit alone does not define a bearing arrangement.
  • Temperature and material. Check how the hole and shaft change size in service, especially with different materials or temperatures.
  • Surface and geometry. Consider finish, coatings, burrs, roundness, straightness and alignment. Diameter limits do not control all of these.
  • Manufacture and inspection. Confirm that the process and measurement method can achieve and verify both specified limits.

The positive gap in an ISO size calculation is not a promise of frictionless movement. Use the calculated limits alongside the drawing's other requirements and the component supplier's application guidance.

06 / FREQUENT QUESTIONS

A few details worth getting right

Is H7/g6 a press fit?

No. For the 1–500 mm range covered here, H7/g6 produces positive dimensional clearance. A press fit requires a different relationship between the hole and shaft limits.

Does “7” mean a 7 µm tolerance?

No. It means IT7. The tolerance width depends on the basic-size interval: an H7 hole is 21 µm wide at 20 mm and 25 µm wide at 50 mm.

Does H7/g6 define the radial gap?

The calculated clearance is diametral. Divide by two only when describing the per-side gap between ideal concentric cylinders. Actual position and geometry affect the local gap.

Can I use the same numbers for an inch fit?

You can convert the resulting limits into inches, but an ISO class does not become an ANSI/ASME B4.1 class through unit conversion. This guide and calculator use metric ISO classes.

References & calculation scope

The notation and fit framework follow ISO 286-1:2010; the tabulated tolerance classes and limit deviations are covered by ISO 286-2:2010. Published WDS hole and shaft tables provide a manufacturer cross-check for the H7 and g6 example deviations. MISUMI's fit guide discusses notation, H7/h6 and application factors.
